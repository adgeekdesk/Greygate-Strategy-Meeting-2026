Finance

Standing Up the Department, Budgeting & Statutory Compliance

Agree the founding mandate of the Finance Department — starting with payment processing and payment-status visibility to Procurement and Projects — plus a minimum-viable monthly P&L reporting cadence, a project-level budget-vs-actual method, and a forward calendar of statutory/financial obligations.

[ Current gap ]

This is the most significant gap identified, and it is structural rather than only procedural. Greygate has no Finance Department. Payments are processed by the CEO alone, with no other function or system recording when a payment has been made — which is why Procurement is regularly slow or strained, and why Projects often cannot tell when a project has actually started. There is no described budgeting or forecasting process for projects or for the business as a whole; profit and loss is not tallied or reported; statutory and financial obligations are not planned for. This creates real risk, particularly with a major capital commitment (the new workshop) approaching.

[ Discussion points ]
  1. 01Agree the Finance Department's founding mandate in priority order: (1) payment processing and payment-status visibility to Procurement and Projects, (2) a minimum-viable monthly management reporting pack, (3) budgeting/forecasting, (4) statutory compliance planning — sequenced so the most urgent bottleneck is fixed first, with the rest following quickly behind.
  2. 02Agree a payment-to-project-start workflow: define that a project is only marked “started” in the system of record once Finance confirms payment, and that this status becomes immediately visible to the Projects Manager, Procurement and Workshop — replacing the current situation where only the CEO knows.
  3. 03Agree a minimum-viable monthly management reporting pack: P&L, cashflow position, and project-level budget-vs-actual, even in a simple spreadsheet form to start.
  4. 04Agree a per-project budgeting method that ties back to the Stage 2 quotation (design cost, elements, fabrication, installation, subcontractor costs, transport/logistics, contingency) so actuals can be reconciled against what was quoted.
  5. 05Build a forward calendar of statutory and financial obligations (tax, licensing, payroll-related statutory payments, insurance renewals, etc.) with owners and lead times.
  6. 06Agree how the new workshop's capital requirement, financing and working-capital impact will be modelled and by when — before further commitments are made.
  7. 07Agree the reporting line: who prepares, who reviews, who presents to the CEO, and on what cycle — and confirm Finance sign-off authority under the Delegation of Authority Matrix agreed in Pillar 1, so not every payment or invoice requires the CEO personally.
