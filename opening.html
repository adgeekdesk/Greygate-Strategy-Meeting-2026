[ Why now ]
  1. 01Greygate marks its 10th anniversary as Zimbabwe's first branding company to move from printed branding into 3D signage, and the first brand identity agency to extend brand expression into the corporate built environment.
  2. 02Over the past decade the business has built the country's only in-house design-to-execution capability spanning architecture, interior architecture, graphic design, signage fabrication, electrical, plumbing, painting, wood artisanry, stonemasonry and welding.
  3. 03Growth has outpaced the systems that support it. The Master SOP documents a sound design-and-build workflow, but several of its own dependencies are not yet functioning consistently in practice: task assignment discipline, project and Gantt planning, inventory and procurement timing, quality control ownership, and time and human-resource management.
  4. 04In parallel, the business has no consistent budgeting or forecasting process, profit and loss is not tallied or reported, and statutory and financial obligations are not planned for in advance.
  5. 05Critical structural gap: Greygate has no Finance Department. Payments — to suppliers, subcontractors, and from clients — are processed by the CEO alone, with no other function or system recording when a payment has been made.
  6. 06Greygate is simultaneously preparing to commission a new workshop and studio roughly six times the size of its current premises. Scaling the physical plant without first correcting the operating model risks scaling the same inefficiencies.
  7. 07This meeting is the first in a series of strategy sessions and is intended to produce a working strategy plan — not merely a discussion.

[ How we will work: OKRs ]A 10th-anniversary inflection point, six-times the physical capacity, a first digital platform

Strategy conversations have historically been framed around KPIs — steady-state metrics that monitor whether business-as-usual is healthy. KPIs stay as ongoing operational dashboards. But a KPI answers “is the machine running normally?” — it does not, by itself, drive the step-change this moment calls for.

This strategy is framed around OKRs — Objectives and Key Results. An Objective is a qualitative, ambitious statement of where a pillar needs to get to. Key Results are the 2–4 measurable, time-bound outcomes that prove it has been achieved.

OKRs are deliberately set to stretch beyond comfortable business-as-usual — achieving roughly 70% of a Key Result is considered a strong result, not a shortfall.

[ A mantra for the 10th anniversary ]

Three directions for the CEO to select from, adapt, or use as a starting point. Each is built from something true about Greygate — its name, its founding “first” story, and its unmatched in-house execution capability.

Built, not just designed“We Don’t Just Design It. We Build It.”

Greygate's core differentiator is its in-house execution team — the only one of its kind in the market. This mantra is a daily reminder to every trade, not only Design, that they carry the brand promise.

The name, reclaimed“Ten Years Through the Gate.”

Plays directly on “Greygate” — a decade of being the gateway through which brands step out of print and into physical space. Works equally as an internal rallying cry and an external anniversary line.

The next decade“First for Ten Years. Built for the Next.”

Honours the founding story (first to move from print to 3D signage, first to take brand identity into the built environment) while pointing forward to the new workshop and the systems being agreed today.
