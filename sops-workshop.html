[ Current gap ]

The Master SOP and its outline are well developed on paper but their status as a governing, adopted document is unclear — there is no stated version-control, training, sign-off or enforcement mechanism, and referenced sub-SOPs (Signage Fabrication, Carpentry, Ironmongery, Wall Dressing, Building, Electronics, Glassworks, Soft Furnishings, Workshop Safety) are named but not yet confirmed as written.

The new premises are roughly six times the current footprint. Expanding physical capacity ahead of fixing task assignment, procurement timing, QC enforcement, the CEO-centred approval bottleneck and financial reporting risks multiplying today's inefficiencies rather than resolving them.

[ Discussion points ]

Policy implementation & SOP rollout

  1. 01Confirm which of the referenced departmental SOPs actually exist today versus still need to be written, and assign an owner and deadline to each gap.
  2. 02Agree a document-control standard: version numbers, approval signatory (PM/CD/CEO), review cycle (e.g. annual or on major change), and a single source location.
  3. 03Agree a rollout method: briefing sessions per department, a sign-off register confirming each staff member has read and understood the SOPs relevant to their role, and refresher training after major changes.
  4. 04Decide which policies are non-negotiable at handover to the new workshop (safety, QC sign-off authority, subcontractor induction) versus which can be phased in.

New workshop & studio — readiness sequencing

  1. 01Agree a sequencing principle: which of the fixes from Sections 7.1–7.5 — including the Finance Department and Delegation of Authority Matrix — must be operating reliably before the new workshop goes live, versus which can mature in parallel.
  2. 02Confirm workforce and skills planning for a larger facility — recruitment, training and supervision ratios (e.g. Workshop Manager span of control) at 6x scale.
  3. 03Confirm how Work Location Rules (Appendix C of the Master SOP — Workshop / Site / Subcontractor Premises) extend to a larger, possibly zoned, workshop layout.
  4. 04Confirm safety, tool-control and QC infrastructure requirements scale with footprint, not just headcount.
