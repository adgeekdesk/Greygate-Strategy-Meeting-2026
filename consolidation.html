[ How OKRs work in this meeting ]
  1. 01Each of the six pillars concludes with a “Decision needed” — this is the raw material for that pillar's Objective.
  2. 02Appendix B captures a draft company-level OKR set for the coming quarter, one Objective per pillar with candidate Key Results, for the CEO and department leads to finalize in this meeting.
  3. 03Within two weeks of this meeting, each department should cascade its own aligned departmental OKRs from the company-level set agreed here.
  4. 04OKRs are reviewed monthly at department level and formally scored (0.0–1.0 per Key Result) at the end of the quarter, feeding directly into the next quarter's strategy session.
  5. 05Existing operational KPIs (QC pass rates, procurement turnaround, on-time delivery) continue to be tracked as health metrics beneath the OKRs — they show whether the business stays stable while the OKRs push it forward.
[ Draft company-level OKRs ]Appendix B · One Objective per pillar

Objective 1 — Organizational Structuring

Establish a clear, accountable operating structure that scales with growth — and end single-person dependency on approvals.

KR 1Interim org chart approved and communicated to 100% of staff
KR 2Finance Department formally established with a named lead and founding mandate
KR 3Delegation of Authority / Approval Matrix approved, removing the CEO as sole approver for Procurement, Planning, Execution, Logistics and Inventory
KR 4CEO and CD override matrices documented and signed off

Objective 2 — Project Planning & Management

Run every active project through a single, visible planning system.

KR 1Odoo (or shortlist) evaluation completed and platform approved
KR 2Pilot module live in one department
KR 3Digital Systems Owner named and onboarded

Objective 3 — Policy Implementation

Make Greygate's policies enforceable, not just documented.

KR 1Document-control standard adopted for all policies and SOPs
KR 2Sign-off register launched, 100% completion by department heads
KR 3Non-negotiable workshop policies confirmed ahead of the new-facility move

Objective 4 — SOPs & Manual Implementation

Make the Master SOP a living, adopted governance document.

KR 1100% of named departmental sub-SOPs confirmed existing, or assigned an owner and drafting deadline
KR 2SOP Programme Owner named
KR 3First round of departmental SOP briefings completed

Objective 5 — Process Flow Improvement

Every task in flight has one owner, one due date, one visible status.

KR 1100% of active Job Cards have a single named task owner and due date
KR 2Interim daily-dashboard fallback in use across all departments
KR 3Escalation triggers documented and tested on at least one live project

Objective 6 — Departmental Efficiency (Procurement, QC & Finance)

Materials, quality and money never bottleneck a project — and everyone who needs to know, knows.

KR 1Payment-to-project-start visibility workflow live: Finance confirms payment, Procurement and Projects are notified automatically
KR 2Procurement approval turnaround meets agreed targets on ≥90% of requests
KR 3Zero fabricated or installed work proceeds without QC sign-off (QC1–QC6) on file
KR 4Monthly management pack (P&L, cashflow, budget-vs-actual) produced within 10 working days of month-end
KR 5Forward statutory/financial obligations calendar live with zero missed deadlines

Scoring note: Key Results are scored 0.0–1.0 at quarter-end. A score around 0.7 on a stretch Key Result is a strong result — the bar is deliberately set above comfortable business-as-usual.
