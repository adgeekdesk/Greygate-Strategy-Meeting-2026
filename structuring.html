[ Current gap ]

The Master SOP defines functional responsibilities well (CEO, PM, Designers, CD, WM, Procurement, Installers, Subcontractors) but does not show a reporting structure, does not name Finance or HR, and does not state escalation paths beyond the CEO/CD override language. In practice, there is no Finance Department at all, and approval authority across Procurement, Planning, Execution, Logistics and Inventory sits with the CEO alone — this is the single biggest structural risk raised for this meeting and is addressed directly in this pillar.

[ Discussion points ]
  1. 01Draft org chart: direct reports into the CEO, and into the Projects Manager, for a 20-person team scaling toward a much larger facility.
  2. 02Stand up a formal Finance Department — not a single named owner bolted onto another role — with an explicit, immediate mandate covering payment processing (accounts payable and receivable), payment-status visibility to Procurement and Projects, budgeting, and statutory compliance. Even a lean structure (one hire plus outsourced bookkeeping/accounting support) resolves the single-point-of-failure risk if the mandate and reporting line are clear.
  3. 03Draft a written Delegation of Authority / Approval Matrix: who may approve what, up to what value, across Procurement, Planning, Execution, Logistics and Inventory — so day-to-day decisions no longer default to the CEO. Reserve CEO approval for defined high-value or high-risk thresholds only, consistent with the CEO Priority Override already described in the Master SOP.
  4. 04Agree a payment-visibility workflow as a first, immediate fix regardless of which digital platform is eventually adopted: the moment a payment is made or received, Procurement and the Projects Manager are notified — by the new Finance function, not solely by the CEO — so procurement can proceed and Projects knows a job has actually started.
  5. 05Decide whether HR/administration is a standalone role or a shared CEO/PM responsibility at current size, with a trigger point (headcount or revenue) for when it becomes standalone.
  6. 06Clarify the CEO Priority Override and Creative Director Override matrices already referenced in the Master SOP — put them in writing with named triggers, not just “CEO may override.”
  7. 07Define who is accountable when a project is late, over-budget, or fails QC — one named accountable owner per project, per the RACI approach already sketched in the SOP outline.
